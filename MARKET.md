# Market study: Machine Slices

One generative machine artwork, divided into 1,000 individually collectible slices that share a runtime. From public sources, read on 2026-10-22.

## Competitors and similar tools

- **Art Blocks** stores generative scripts and token seeds on Ethereum and provides infrastructure for reconstructing browser-viewable art from onchain data. This is a preservation precedent; its general protocol description does not establish a collection consisting of 1,000 spatial slices of one shared machine. [1]
- **Async Art** supported a master artwork composed of individually owned, controllable layers. Its restoration project reports that the original infrastructure going offline prevented compositing and layer control, and that some missing IPFS assets remain a limitation. Its documented model concerns controllable layers rather than the fixed spatial slices proposed here. [2]
- **Chromology**, by Fabin Rasheed, uses collector-controlled expression and theme layers for participatory generative art. Its artist describes an open-ended drawing experience rather than a single machine divided into a fixed grid. [3]

## Who it is for

The proposed audience is collectors of generative art and viewers interested in code-based compositions. This is an inference from the comparable projects, not measured demand for Machine Slices. Art Blocks explicitly addresses artists and collectors through its preservation architecture; Chromology describes collaboration between artist and audience. [1][3]

## What is missing

The proposed distinction is spatial ownership within one coherent moving artwork: a slice should work as an individual composition while revealing its place in the whole. None of the sources establishes that this exact idea is absent from the wider market, or that buyers want 1,000 editions. Treat differentiation as an artistic proposition to demonstrate, not a proven market gap. [1–3]

Preservation should be demonstrable: publish a self-contained renderer and reconstruct the work from onchain data without a proprietary website or external asset host. This recommendation follows Art Blocks' reconstruction model and Async's documented restoration problems. [1][2]

## Who would pay, and why

Prospective buyers are collectors who value the composition and the relationship between a fragment and the complete work. That motivation is a hypothesis; these sources establish no willingness to pay, price, or likely sales for this project. Prioritise a convincing whole-work demonstration and compelling individual slices before deciding distribution terms. [1–3]
